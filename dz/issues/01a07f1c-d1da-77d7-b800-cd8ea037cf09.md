`STATUS_B` is not a machine flag. The 6502 has no B bit in the status
register; it exists only in the byte `PHP` and an interrupt push, and it is
discarded on the way back in. The IR models it as a `CPUReg8` anyway.

Nothing reads it. Counting `LoadR8 STATUS_B` in `--ir --no-ir-trees` at -O3:

    rom        0 loads, 14 stores
    robotron   0 loads, 13 stores
    snake-byte 0 loads, 11 stores
    bolo       0 loads, 11 stores

The stores are all synthetic: `GenIR.cpp` seeds it from the recorded entry
state, and `emitDecodeFlags()` writes it 0. `emitEncodeFlags()` does not
even include it -- `PHP` builds its byte from C, Z, I, D, V and N, and
`IRC1.cpp` ORs in `STATUS_B` at print time -- so the one place the bit is
supposed to matter does not go through the register.

Yet `--dump-reg-liveness` reports it live-out in 8 to 16 functions per
corpus and live-in in 1 to 9. With zero readers in the module, every one of
those is contamination that has propagated out of an all-ones block (see the
blind-call-site bug) and then been intersected with a `modified` set that
contains B only because of those synthetic stores.

It costs a bit of every liveness set and it puts a column of pure noise in
front of anyone reading the dump. Either drop the register and let `PHP`
synthesise the bit the way `IRC1` already does, or keep it and exclude it
from the liveness sets explicitly. Until then, treat any B in a liveness
result as meaningless -- `--dump-reg-liveness` says so in its legend.
