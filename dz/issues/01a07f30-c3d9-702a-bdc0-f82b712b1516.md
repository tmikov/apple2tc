`probe_take_snapshot()` (`lib/a2host/probe.c:107`) writes the 64K RAM image,
the registers, the cycle counter and -- deliberately -- the soft switches, on
the stated grounds that they are

> the half of the machine a RAM image cannot carry: they are not memory, they
> are host IO state.

Today that means `vid_control` and the keyboard latch. When the game port
lands (dz `01a074d9-9034-72fe-9efe-87de198c6dd6`) there will be more state in
exactly that category and the snapshot will not carry it: the four paddle
positions, the three button states, and any one-shot still in flight.

## Consequence

A snapshot captured at a joystick-dependent entry point cannot reconstruct the
stick from RAM, so a run rebased on it resumes with a centred stick and
nothing pressed. For a program that reads the port on its first frame, that is
a different machine from the one that was captured.

This does not affect the game port's own calibration test, which reads result
bytes out of the RAM image rather than restoring a machine.

## Why it is filed separately

Adding the fields changes the snapshot register-file format, which has its own
compatibility question -- anything parsing those files has to tolerate the new
lines. `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-07-game-port-paddles-design.md` records
the omission as a known limitation rather than growing to cover a format
change, and points here.

## Wanted

Either the fields, written in the same raw-and-decoded style as the video
state:

- `paddle0`-`paddle3`, the positions;
- the button states;
- whether a one-shot is in flight at the capture point and how much of it
  remains, which unlike the others is time-relative and may be better
  expressed as "expires N cycles after capture" than as an absolute.

...or a decision that snapshots deliberately capture the machine with input at
rest, documented at `probe_take_snapshot()` next to the comment that already
explains why the soft switches are there.
